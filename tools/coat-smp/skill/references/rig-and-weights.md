## 1. Coordinates and the survey
Skyrim units (1 unit ~ 1.43 cm), z up: ground ~0, knee ~33, pelvis ~68, belt ~70-74, hem of a knee skirt 35-45. The actor faces **+y**; **+x is the actor's RIGHT** (`NPC L Thigh` sits at x ~ -6.6). Angles round the body: atan2(x - cx, y - cy) in degrees, 0 = front, +90 = actor's right, 180 = back. When the user describes a screenshot, map image left/right to the actor's right/left before acting.

Survey before designing (`scripts/survey_mesh.py`): shapes, which bones already move, **welded components** (pieces joined by shared points, UV seams welded) and **texture islands**. Moving cloth is often fused into a bigger shape (a skirt welded to the torso; 3BA women's sets are often one shape split only by texture islands). Look for: slits (uncovered angular spans at the hem) and their apex heights; the belt's lower edge per angle; rest gap from the cloth to the trousers; single- vs double-walled cloth; hidden junk (a hidden disc through both thighs, an exact duplicate belt left by a previous patch, zero-area slivers). Put the sizes you rely on in `expect=` so the build stops if the mesh ever changes.

Claude cannot see 3D: render it (`pose_render.py`; front/side views) and read the numbers.

**Assigning roles from the survey:**
| Role | How to recognise it |
|---|---|
| `skirt:outer` | the visible skirt layer that meets the legs; bottom edge 35-55, top hidden under the belt / cuirass; exactly one |
| `skirt:inner` | another skirt layer under the outer one (cloth under mail); same height range, smaller radius |
| `belt` | a band round the waist (z ~63-85) with a hanging tail (its component reaching lowest, usually at the front); a bag = its biggest other component (`bag=True`) |
| `flaps` | hip plates / tassets: two large, equal components at about +-90 deg, hanging over the skirt |
| `pants` | the trousers: textured, runs from the ankles (z < 15) up to the hips |
| `static` | the cuirass and anything else rigid; the lowest one over the skirt's top sets `sk_belt` |
| `drop` | no texture = an old collision body (`coll_body`, `Under...`) or hidden underwear; the builder writes its own `VirtualGround` |
If the source already has an SMP rig (moving bones, an HDT link): leave its bones alone - bones the builder does not know are folded into the pelvis on rigid shapes, the pieces get the new rig, and the new HDT link replaces the old one (the old XML is simply unused; a separate old SMP patch mod providing the same mesh must lose priority or be disabled). Drop the old rig's collision shapes. Name pieces after the source shapes (`('Saia', 'Saia', None, 'skirt:outer')`) unless you cut several pieces out of one shape.

## 2. Pieces
- Cut the moving cloth into its own shape(s). Every drawn shape has an **80-bone cap** (more crashes the game) and collisions are per piece. Seam points keep identical weights in both pieces; a piece cut out of a bigger shape fades physics to 0 over ~4 units at the cut (`SEAM_FADE`), so the seam never opens.
- Low-poly cloth only bends at its vertex rows: **midpoint-subdivide** moving pieces (1 -> 4 per triangle; new points lie exactly on the old surface, so it looks identical at rest). Collide with hidden copies of the original (or split-once) triangles to keep physics cost down.
- Drop the author's zero-area slivers before subdividing (they become inside-out normals).
- Texture-swap variants (ARMA alternate textures) match shapes by NAME: new split shapes need their own swap entries.

## 3. Rig layouts
**Skirt (bundled builder):** N chains round the body, each a pinned anchor parented to `NPC Pelvis [Pelv]` plus 5 swinging bones placed on the cloth surface from the belt to the hem. Converged recipe: **12 chains x 5 bones**. Chains sit in panels between the outer layer's slits; neighbours inside a panel are linked (constraint groups with frameInLerp 0.5), neighbours across a slit only above the slit apex. Every cloth point blends 2 chains x 2 rows - a point on one bone only creases ("I can see the skeleton").

**Floor-length gown / any dress over bare legs (bundled builder, 2026-10-11, in-game check pending):** design keys
`nrow=7, max_chains=10` (10 x 7 + leg bones stays under 80), `free_max=1.0` (a bell hanging clear of the legs is 100%
physics - at 0.9 the calves dragged 8-11% of the hem and a run stretched it), `free_sigma=6` (wider thigh -> swing hand-over),
`leg_zmin` ~6 (calves + ankles push the hem); per target `legs_body=dict(osp, set, shape)` = the legs come from the body's
own BodySlide set (an outfit project usually has the thighs cut away under the skirt), its slider data goes onto the leg
colliders (only sliders the dress set has) and verify_rig.py checks them against the body; `ground_z` ~ hem - 1.5 (a hem
resting on z 0 touches the default ground plane at rest). Measure edge stretch in leg poses against the author's rig
before installing (a static mesh on thigh weights stretches 4-19x p99 in a run; a good gown rig ~0.2-0.4).
A dense dress rig (18 x 8 bones) needs `panels=3` (skirt cut into shapes by chain sector for the 80-bone cap), `lining_lr=True`,
soft `links` with springs, `row_margin` growing towards the hem and `thigh_leash` - every key is explained in
`scripts/designs/example_design.py`. Dresses are NOT armour skirts: never start them from the armour presets (stiff, no drape);
start from a dress rig the user likes and translate its numbers.

**Layered skirt (mail over cloth):** ONE shared rig for all layers - two layers on separate rigs collide at rest and twitch. Judge gaps and collisions on the OUTER visible layer; inner layers ride the same chains.

**Rigid hip plates / tassets:** each plate on its own bone hinged at its top edge; plate points follow the skirt right under them 95% (a bigger own share swings out of step after a stop and dips the plate into the skirt); protect that small own share in the 4-influence cap. Straps fade from the belt (top) to the plate (bottom).

**Belt tail / strap:** own 2-4 bone chain. Weight stacked layers (braid + grommets) **by height alone**, never nearest-vertex copies (layers separate). Blend from the band over ~4 units (shorter bends it sharply under the buckle). Ride the surface under it ~35%: 90% looks frozen. No collider on a piece that mostly rides the bones of what it lies on.

**Bag:** own bone hinged at the belt; straps fade belt -> bag.

**Front flap (loincloth):** ONE chain hinged at the BELT (not under the belt decorations - a hinge low on the upper thigh cannot clear a walking thigh at any angle); decorations ride it; barely any back swing (it folds back between the legs and sticks); collides only with the forward-facing leg triangles, penetration 3.

**Tabard panels:** per panel 3 chains x (anchor + 5), links only inside a panel. Over a leg-bound mail layer, drape the tabard partly onto the legs - **uniformly across a panel at each height**, or stances shear diagonal folds.

**Cloak / cape:** ~7 chains x (anchor under `NPC Spine2 [Spn2]` + 8), side-linked; the mantle keeps the author's weights and physics ramps in over ~10 units below the shoulders. Inward limit 0, friction 0 (so it slides off the body).

**Pendulum (a heavy chain or ornament on a cloak):** fixed anchor above the chain's middle pinned to the upper spine + one heavy swinging bone at the centre (mass ~10x the cloak, low inertia, damped); 0% at the clasps rising to ~90% in the middle.

Bone names: `<PREFIX> Skirt<chain:02d> <row>` etc. **Unique prefix per armour family** - SMP finds bones by name on the actor; two armours with the same bone names tangle on swap. Never put physics on the head bone.

## 4. Weights: the hybrid recipe
`w = (1 - share) * leg_follow + share * physics_chain`, then <= 4 influences, renormalised.
- **share** ramps from 0 at the belt to its max over RAMP units (6-12), times FREE(gap): cloth lying on the trousers follows them exactly; cloth hanging clear swings. GAP near/far: tight tunic 0.8/5.5, free bell 0.5/3.0, mail 0.5/3.5, short hip-hugging skirts 0/1.5, a flap in front of the thighs 0/6. FREE_MAX ~0.8-0.9: a fully free skirt is outrun by a sprint stride and left behind the thigh.
- **leg_follow** comes from ONE smooth source: the trousers' own weights at the nearest surface point, blending to a sharp inverse-distance mix further off; vanilla `SkirtF/B/L/RBone*` folded into the pelvis, calves folded into thighs, Gaussian-smoothed (~3 units). Mixing sources (plates 100% thigh + straps half on a vanilla skirt bone) tore neighbouring cloth points apart.
- At the front and back centre blend the left and right thighs smoothly over a wide angle (~60 deg); a narrow handover tears a step into the cloth when the legs stand apart.
- **Physics chain part:** lateral bracket between neighbouring chains x vertical bracket between rows; row 0 maps to the pelvis, not the anchor.
- Cloth's two faces at a cut edge (within ~0.35-0.5) share averaged weights.

## 5. The 4-influence cap: merge, never drop
Each vertex may have 4 bones. Dropping the smallest weight is what tears cloth: dropping the left thigh on one side of a centre line and the right on the other made a 260-415% stretch step at a slit. Merge the smallest into the bone that moves most like it: a physics bone into the same chain, a calf into its thigh, a thigh into the pelvis; protect thighs/pelvis in the centre zones and a plate's own small share. **Key any "is this a physics bone" test on a parameter, not one armour's prefix** - a helper keyed on one prefix silently merged another armour's physics into the thighs.

## 6. Colliders
Hidden colliders are extra skinned shapes in the same NIF with **no shader** (never rendered), weighted to body bones (or copied from the visible points they were made from, so they move identically).
- **Legs: the real trousers surface, never pulled inside the legs** (a 3-unit pull-in = the thigh travels 3 units into the skirt before anything pushes). Split into **three one-sided pieces by facing**: front (normal y > 0.25), back (y < -0.25), side (|x| > 0.6); front/back penetration 3, side 1.5. Split the skirt lining the same way by angle (within 55 deg of front / back, else side) and let each lining piece collide only with its matching leg piece: however deep a fast thigh goes, the skirt is pushed back out the way it came (a closed tube pushed it out the back wall = "stuck behind the leg"; 44 hooked points -> 0).
- **Never the inner thigh faces**: drop every triangle whose normal points at the body's centre line by > 0.6 from ALL pieces (they sit 1.5 units apart at the crotch and pinch whatever falls between the legs). Points that end up between the thighs then hang there unseen - by design, hidden inside the trousers.
- **Cloth the author buried in the trousers must not collide** (it follows the legs anyway): collide only points resting >= 0.6 off the real trousers in every BodySlide state, and pull the collider in 1.0 at most to clear the rest. Decide the colliding set once on `_1`.
- **Test on the game's own clips**, not only hand poses: decode the actor's walk / run / sprint / jump-landing / attack hkx (PyNifly's Skyrim reader), move the leg pieces per frame with the skirt held at rest, and count entered / caught / hooked (pushed at the centre line) / pinched (two opposing pushes) / unseen per clip. A sprint swings the thigh 83 deg, a jump landing or a power attack over 100 - far beyond the hand poses.
- Boots: copy the real boot surface (cylinders sat 1.5-5 units inside chunky boots).
- Cloth layers: an inner layer's points vs the outer cloth's inward lining; hanging pieces (flaps, bag, tail) vs the skirt's outer surface (pulled in so nothing touches at rest).
- Ground plane `VirtualGround` (4 points at +-80, z ~0, weighted 100% to `NPC Root [Root]`).
- BodySlide body copies are ~7x denser at the calves: decimate by vertex clustering.

## 7. _0 / _1 meshes and BodySlide variants
- The game blends `_0` and `_1` **per vertex by index**: identical topology, bones, splits AND hidden collider point counts. Compute weights, splits and every angle/facing decision once on `_1`, reuse for `_0`. If the `_1` trousers carry extra pieces the `_0` lacks, build leg colliders only from the shared part (`pants_n`).
- BodySlide projects: run the SAME builder on the set's ShapeData (zero-slider) mesh, record each written point's provenance and give it slider data (re-sliced for split shapes, mean of the two parents for subdivision midpoints, the source points' data for colliders). See pipeline-and-packaging.md.
